# 16 HR interview: Deanna Tung

HR practitioner, ran the 2026-10-19 call. Full text: [transcript](reference/interviews/2026-10-19-deanna-tung-hr-practitioner.txt). This is one person's opinion, not data. Cite it as "one HR practitioner". She says the team must talk to more HR people before 23 Oct and will send names.

## What she told us (evidence for slides 2, 3 and 8)

- **Today's process.** Her group (about 10 companies, 3 active) runs monthly Google Forms for 3 months of probation. Buddy rates new hire. New hire rates buddy. Boss rates new hire. New hire rates boss. Forms are not anonymous. Only she reads them. No red flag: she writes "no red flags" in the record.
- **Why she built it.** To find problems before probation ends, not after the person resigns.
- **Why new hires leave.** Main reason: the job is not what they were told. Gen Z has low patience. Sales has the highest turnover, about 30% leave in 1 to 3 years (her estimate, not computed).
- **Belonging is real.** Example: a client with 95% Mandarin speakers. New hires from other groups felt shut out and disengaged.
- **Workload** is a later problem, not a new-hire problem.

## What to change or build

Ordered by how clear her advice was.

1. **Plain words on the Impact page.** "8 points above baseline" confused her. Rewrite labels for a non-technical HR person. Smallest change, high value.
2. **Order of problem areas.** Put Workload last. Keep Belonging prominent.
3. **Pitch flow.** She said the demo jumped around. Pick one path: new hire joins, check-in, replies grouped, HR fix, recheck, result. Match it to the 10-slide structure in [deliverables/10](../deliverables/10-presentation-slides/README.md).
4. **Serious concerns.** She confirmed the current design: detect keywords, give an official complaint channel, tell HR. She added that HR cannot act without a name, so say so plainly. Also show HR a count of "someone felt unsafe" even when no one gives a name. Check this against R13, R14 and [14](14-ai-use-contract.md) before changing anything.
5. **Ongoing check-ins for all staff.** Her best idea. Add repeat check-ins at 6 months, 1 year and so on, not only new hires. Scheduling is out of scope today. Pitch it on slide 10 as the next step. Build it only if [00](00-intent.md) allows.
6. **Handbook bot must be clever.** She hates bots that solve nothing. Show a real answer with the handbook quote. Show what the bot does when the handbook has no answer (it goes to HR).
7. **Job-match card.** She said job match is Challenge 2, not ours, because the person is already hired. Our R52 card shows which roles oversell. Keep it small. Frame it as "what new hires say the job is", never as a hiring tool.
8. **Buddy and boss ratings.** Her group rates both ways. A possible add: new hire rates their manager inside the check-in. Not built. Check privacy rules before adding.

## Second call: pitch rehearsal, 4 Nov 2026

Full text: [transcript](reference/interviews/2026-11-04-deanna-tung-pitch-rehearsal.txt). She is a judge on the final day.

What she said:

- **Hear both sides.** Sapa listens only to the new hire, so the manager looks like the bad guy. HR must also hear the immediate superior, "just like when we conduct domestic inquiries … we cannot listen to one side". Buyers are employers, so the product must be fair to both.
- **The manager does not know the issue.** A fix that says "schedule a one-to-one" leaves the manager asking "a one-to-one about what?"
- **Offer a meeting.** Ask the new hire "would you like to arrange a meeting with your supervisor?" If yes, it goes to the supervisor.
- Say "HR practitioners", not "HR people". Tell judges about the QR code at the start.

What we decided (owner, 4 Nov 2026): the team lead's side (R66, R67), "Tell my team lead" (R65) and the actionable fix (R68) in [05](05-implementation-spec.md).
