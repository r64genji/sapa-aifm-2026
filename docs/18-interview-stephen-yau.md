# 18 HR interview: Stephen Yau Kah Yoon

Transformation & Change Management Officer, Setia Awan Land Sdn Bhd. Call on 2026-10-21. Full text: [transcript](reference/interviews/2026-10-21-stephen-yau-transformation-officer.txt). He agreed to be named and quoted in the pitch. One person's opinion, not data.

## What he told us

- The most common reason new hires leave is bad onboarding. Firms throw people into work and say they have no time for orientation.
- Next is wrong fit: culture shock, or no chemistry with the superior.
- A new hire must be very clear on the job description (R and R). Some firms write the JD one way and the superior asks for another.
- He keeps onboarding open until confirmation, about 6 months. Heads of department often skip follow-up because they are "very busy". HR still does it.
- Today's check is a short interview at 3 months and a pulse survey at 6 months, usually on a Google Form. HR reads it by hand and tells the head of department.
- Firms usually know before someone resigns, because staff give feedback.
- Rhythm: weekly in month 1 (the crucial month), every 2 weeks in month 2, then less often.
- Workload and superior-support questions are common in company-wide pulse surveys, about every 6 months. He sees no problem asking them of all staff.
- Response to pulse surveys is over 90%. It is about 70% before a reminder and close to 90% after.
- **Anonymity:** for a new joiner, drop it. A lone joiner in a department (his example: cost and contract) cannot hide, and HR must know the truth to go straight to that superior. Each department has its own problem (superior in one, unclear JD in another). Onboarding checks do not look at anonymity.
