# Sapa — Adversarial Review of Architecture and Product

*As of 14 Oct 2026. Reviews [01](../01-product-rundown-and-deliverables.md), [02](../02-prototype-architecture.md) and [03](../03-research-dossier.md). Every finding names the claim, why it breaks, and the fix. The fixes are folded into the [build spec](../05-implementation-spec.md).*

**Verdict.** The product idea fits Challenge 4 well, and the privacy thinking is above hackathon level. But the plan has three problems a sharp judge or a bad demo day can expose:

1. **The headline feature rarely fires for the target customer.** With a 5-person minimum and realistic response rates, a monthly intake of 5 hires almost never shows a theme.
2. **Some privacy promises are weaker than the docs say.** Live counters let a manager see who said what. Service-role code bypasses RLS. Traces and gateway logs keep copies that DELETE does not reach.
3. **The demo path has avoidable moving parts.** A home server sits on it, and several demo steps are not yet designed (the recheck question, manager Telegram binding, the seeded timeline).

None of this needs a new idea. Most fixes delete things.

Severity: **S1** = can break the demo or the core claim. **S2** = a judge can catch it, or it costs a day. **S3** = tidy-up.

## Part 1 — Product

### P1 (S1). The 5-person minimum and the target customer do not fit

**Claim.** Target is 100–1,000 staff with monthly intakes. Demo company: 60 hires a year, about 5 a month. Themes appear only at 5+ respondents.

**Problem.** 5 hires a month at the target 60% response rate is 3 respondents. The theme view is suppressed in most months. The dossier says "below 5, data is pooled across intakes", but the architecture has no pooling. So the cohort page, the core "AI finds themes" screen, is blank for the primary customer in real use. The demo hides this: 5 of 6 seeded hires replied (83%).

**Fix.**
- Make the default theme unit a **rolling window** ("hires who started in the last 90 days"), not a single intake. Intake view stays as a drill-down when it meets the minimum.
- State it on the slide: "Themes need 5 people. A 5-hire intake gets themes from a rolling 90-day window."
- Admit that for small companies the real day-one value is the **flag queue** (individual replies to HR), not themes.

### P2 (S1). Live counters break the 5-person promise

**Claim.** "Themes only from 5+ people" protects identity.

**Problem.** The demo's own climax is the counter moving from 3 to 4 seconds after one person replies. Anyone watching a live counter learns that person's tag. A manager who knows Aisyah had her Day 14 check-in today can read her answer off the counter. This is a classic differencing attack (learn one person's data by comparing two totals). The minimum-group rule does not stop it.

**Fix.**
- Live counters are for **HR only**. HR can already read individual replies, so live updates show HR nothing new.
- Managers see **frozen weekly snapshots**, never live counts.
- In any manager view, a theme count under 3 shows as "fewer than 3".

### P3 (S1). An action can expose the person it is meant to protect

**Claim.** Replies are HR-only by default. The action goes to the supervisor.

**Problem.** If Raj supervises one new hire, "agree a weekly priority list with your new hire" tells Raj exactly what that hire said. The architecture lets HR draft an action from one person's reply and assign it to that person's manager. The default "HR only" then means nothing.

**Fix.** Two rules, enforced in code:
- An action built from replies of people who did not consent to share must be **team-level** ("for all new hires in your team"). It must not name a person.
- If the manager has fewer than 3 new hires in scope, the system blocks assigning a non-consented, person-derived action to that manager. HR must own it or make it skip-level.

### P4 (S2). "Are your priorities clearer?" is not designed

**Claim.** The loop closes when the next check-in asks the recheck question.

**Problem.** The schedule is fixed touchpoints with a fixed question bank. Nothing links an action to a later question. The demo's last step has no data model behind it.

**Fix.** Add a `recheck` link on each action: when an action reaches *done*, the system queues one recheck question into the next check-in of each person in the action's scope. The answer is tagged against the action, and the action card shows "Recheck: 3 of 4 say clearer".

### P5 (S2). The only unreviewed AI output goes straight to the employee

**Claim.** "Every AI step lands in a human review queue."

**Problem.** The follow-up question is generated by the model and sent to the new hire with no human check. It is the one AI output that a vulnerable user sees directly. A leading or odd question ("Do you feel like quitting?") in front of judges is a real risk. It also breaks the dossier's own "AI as a fixed pipeline" rule.

**Fix.** Do not generate follow-up text. Keep a fixed bank of about 6 neutral probes per language ("Could you tell me a bit more?", "What would help with that?"). The classifier picks **whether** to probe and **which** probe ID. The AI still decides; it just cannot write free text to the employee. This also deletes one prompt and one failure mode.

### P6 (S2). Counting is done by the model

**Claim.** Step B "outputs counted themes ('3 of 5')".

**Problem.** LLMs miscount. A wrong number on stage, next to a "4 of 6" that SQL already shows, is a credibility hit. Also, the live counter must update in seconds, and Step B is nightly.

**Fix.** All counts come from SQL over reviewed tags. Step B only writes a short narrative from those numbers, and the narrative must not contain any number that is not in the input.

### P7 (S2). The "Don't contact me" button may conflict with the law the dossier cites

**Claim.** Escalation offers "Don't contact me" and cites Employment Act s.81B (duty to inquire into a sexual harassment complaint within 30 days).

**Problem.** If a chat disclosure to the employer's HR counts as a complaint, HR may have a duty to act whatever the button says. The bot must not imply that pressing it stops HR from acting.

**Fix.** Bot text says: "HR will see this. You can ask HR not to contact you now. For some serious matters HR may still need to follow up." Raise it with the organisers' HR judges as a known open question rather than hide it.

### P8 (S2). Evidence numbers that judges can pull apart

- **Escalation recall "100%" on 8–10 cases.** With 10 of 10 caught, the 95% confidence interval still goes down to about 69%. Say "10 of 10 in our test set" and give the interval.
- **Labellers wrote the replies.** People who write test replies know the intended label. Agreement will look too good. Fix: the person who did not write a reply labels it, and prompt tuning uses a separate dev split from the reported test split.
- **Cohen's κ on multi-label drivers** is not one number. Report κ per driver as yes/no, then the mean.
- **Test 3 (time to insight).** The Sapa tester sees the AI's answer, the spreadsheet tester does not. Fine, that is the point, but the person must not be the one who planted the themes.
- **ROI.** "3 of 15 exits prevented" has no evidence behind it. Lead with break-even (0.36 exits a year) and present the rest as scenarios.
- **Pay** is the top stated reason for leaving (53%). Sapa does not touch it. Say so on slide 10 before a judge does.

### P9 (S2). The WhatsApp story is bigger than "a config change"

**Claim.** Chat SDK makes WhatsApp "a config change you can show in Q&A".

**Problem.** Scheduled check-ins start a conversation. On WhatsApp that needs pre-approved template messages, Meta business verification and a per-message fee. The architecture says this in 4.1, but the pitch line oversells it. The dossier also lists "which app do frontline workers actually use?" as unanswered, and that question decides whether the frontline claim holds.

**Fix.** Pitch line: "Same bot logic runs on WhatsApp. Scheduled messages there need Meta-approved templates, which is a setup step, not a rewrite." Answer the app-use question in the HR interviews.

### P10 (S3). Smaller product notes

- "Pseudonymous" person records are not pseudonymous to HR, who must follow up escalations. Say "confidential", which the dossier already does in F9.
- Denominator for "4 of 6" is unclear (hires or respondents). Fix the wording to "4 of 6 who answered".
- The state name "approved" after "assigned" is odd: the manager is accepting the task, not approving it. Use *accepted*.

## Part 2 — Architecture

### A1 (S1). A home server on the demo path, for no security gain

**Claim.** OmniRoute on a home server over Tailscale Funnel keeps keys off Vercel.

**Problem.**
- The OmniRoute key and the direct Gemini fallback key both sit in Vercel anyway. Nothing stays off Vercel.
- The home server, home ISP and Funnel are the only non-managed links on the demo path. The doc admits this is the biggest risk.
- Every failure costs 8 seconds before fallback. On stage, that is a frozen phone.
- OmniRoute keeps request logs on the home server. Those are copies of employee text that DELETE and retention never reach (see A6).
- Day 1 already has a task just to check whether OmniRoute breaks JSON-schema requests.

**Fix.** Remove OmniRoute from the app. Call `gemini-3.8-flash` through one managed route with no home hardware. Two options:

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Vercel AI Gateway** (recommended) | Already on Vercel, auth by OIDC, provider fallback, usage view, zero-data-retention option. Lists `gemini-3.8-flash` at the same price | Small free credit; costs past that |
| Direct Gemini API key | Fewest parts | No usage view; you build the fallback yourself |

Keep OmniRoute for the team's own dev use if you want, not in production.

**Team decision (14 Oct).** Keep OmniRoute, exposed with a `cloudflared` named tunnel and an app-only API key. This keeps the Gemini keys off Vercel. The risks above still apply, so the spec adds: a direct-Gemini fallback, OmniRoute logging off for this key, a status light on the demo page, and a day-1 test that the fallback works with the tunnel stopped.

### A2 (S1). App-level encryption and Supabase Realtime do not mix

**Claim.** Reply text is AES-256-GCM encrypted in the app. Realtime pushes new replies to the dashboard.

**Problem.** Realtime sends database row changes to the browser. Those rows hold ciphertext. The browser has no key, so the "reply appears on screen" moment shows nothing readable. Putting the key in the browser defeats the encryption.

**Fix.** Use Realtime as a **signal only**. The browser listens for "something changed in cohort X" on a table that holds no text, then asks the server for fresh data. The server decrypts, checks the role, logs the read, and returns it. This also fixes A3.

### A3 (S1). "Every read passes RLS" and "every view is logged" are both false as designed

**Problem.**
- The bot webhook, dispatcher and AI pipeline must use the Supabase service-role key. That key bypasses RLS. So RLS protects dashboard users only, not the server code.
- If the dashboard reads through `supabase-js` in the browser, reads go straight to the database. The app never sees them, so it cannot log them.

**Fix.**
- Say it straight: "RLS limits what each HR or manager login can read. Server jobs run as a trusted service and are audited."
- All reads of reply text go through server code, which decrypts and writes the audit row. The browser never queries text tables directly.
- Keep RLS on as a second wall, so a bug in the server code that uses the user's session still cannot leak data.

### A4 (S1). Webhook handling will double-process or time out

**Problem.** Telegram resends an update (same `update_id`) when it does not get a quick 200. The handler waits on an AI call with an 8 s timeout plus a fallback. Slow replies cause resends, which cause double tags, double follow-ups, and possibly a double escalation alert. After repeated failures Telegram can disable the webhook.

**Fix.**
- Store `update_id` in Postgres with a unique key. A repeat insert means "already seen": return 200 and stop.
- Return 200 at once. Do the AI work after the response (`after()` in Next.js) on Fluid Compute.
- Check the webhook secret header on every call.

### A5 (S2). The demo timeline does not add up

**Problem.**
- "6 hires at Day 13, 5 of whom have already replied" — replied to what? Day 14 has not been sent at Day 13. If the 5 replied to Day 14, they are not at Day 13.
- The demo clock is one offset for the whole organisation. Pressing "Advance" also makes the 5 seeded hires due. Their chat IDs are fake, so sends fail or, worse, go to a real chat.
- Working hours and public holidays (F3 rule) are not in the data model. Malaysia has state-specific holidays.

**Fix.**
- Seed the 5 synthetic hires with start dates 1–3 days earlier, so their Day 14 check-ins are already answered. Only the live hire is at Day 13.
- Add `is_synthetic` to people. The dispatcher never calls Telegram for them.
- Store an organisation time zone (`Asia/Kuala_Lumpur`) and a holiday list. Demo mode skips the working-hours rule.

### A6 (S2). Copies of employee text outside the delete path

**Problem.** DELETE removes replies and tags from Postgres. But copies live in: Langfuse traces (input and output text), OmniRoute logs, Chat SDK state tables, Vercel function logs if text is ever logged, and Gemini if the key is on a free tier (free-tier data can be used to improve Google products).

**Fix.**
- Do not send reply text to any trace store. If you keep tracing, record IDs, token counts, model, prompt version, latency and schema-valid yes/no only.
- With A1, OmniRoute is gone.
- DELETE also clears the person's chat-state rows.
- Never log message text. Add a lint rule or a code-review check.
- Use a paid Gemini route or the gateway's zero-retention setting for anything beyond synthetic data.

### A7 (S2). Too many layers for three people and eight days

Fourteen layers, five of them hosted services with their own dashboards and keys. Each one is a place a teammate gets stuck.

**Fix — delete:**
- **OmniRoute and Tailscale Funnel** (A1).
- **Langfuse.** The eval is a script that writes a results file and a chart. Model and prompt version already sit on each tag row. Add it back after the pitch if wanted.
- **Generated follow-ups** (P5).

That leaves: Next.js on Vercel, Supabase (Postgres, Auth, RLS, Realtime, Cron), Chat SDK with the Telegram adapter, AI SDK with Zod, shadcn/ui, and one model route.

### A8 (S2). Manager Telegram is assumed, not designed

**Problem.** The demo has the supervisor approve in Telegram. The architecture has managers in the web app, and the bot only knows new hires' chat IDs. There is no manager binding, and Telegram button data is limited to 64 bytes.

**Fix.** A manager gets their own one-time link to bind Telegram to their login. Action alerts carry two buttons, *Accept* and *Mark done*, and a link to the web card. Button data is a short action ID plus a verb.

### A9 (S3). Smaller architecture notes

- **Evidence check vs masking.** The model sees masked text, so its quote holds placeholders. Compare the quote to the masked text, after normalising whitespace and Unicode. Show HR the matching span in the real text.
- **DELETE "keeps anonymous counts."** Counts are computed live from tags, so deleting tags lowers them. Pick one rule. Recommended: deletion removes the person from all live counts. Weekly snapshots already published stay as they are.
- **Per-minute cron.** Fine. It must call the production URL, because Vercel preview URLs sit behind login.
- **Roles in `app_metadata`.** Needs an admin path to set them. Seed script for the demo; a settings page later.
- **AI SDK 7** needs Node 22+ and ESM. Check its API against the installed docs, not older examples.
- **Key rotation** for the encryption key is not planned. Fine for the prototype; note it as a known gap.

## Part 3 — What holds up

These parts are strong. Keep them as they are.

- Commands (SKIP, STOP, DELETE) handled before any AI call.
- Output schema with no emotion, personality or quit-risk field.
- Evidence quote checked as an exact substring.
- Keyword safety list running beside the model, either one escalates.
- Escalations in their own HR-only table, excluded from summaries.
- Check-ins as rows with a movable clock, not sleeping workflows.
- Fixed taxonomy instead of clustering.
- No agent framework.
- Honest data notes in the dossier (M).

## Part 4 — Decisions for the team

1. **Model route:** decided. OmniRoute behind `cloudflared`, with a direct-Gemini fallback (see A1).
2. **Langfuse:** drop for the competition (recommended), or keep with text-free traces only.
