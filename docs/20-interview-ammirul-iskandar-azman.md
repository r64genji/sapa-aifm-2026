# 20 HR interview: Ammirul Iskandar Azman

Business Development Executive at Gem-Asia Sdn Bhd. His own words: he holds an HR management degree and consults for companies, but does not work inside an HR department. He also says his firm builds a similar HR platform. Call on 2026-10-23. Full text: [transcript](reference/interviews/2026-10-23-ammirul-iskandar-azman-hr-consultant.txt). The transcript labels swap between speakers in places. He agreed to be named and quoted in the pitch. One person's opinion, not data.

## What he told us

- **Today's process.** Probation is 6 months for permanent staff (junior exec and above). It is 1 to 3 months for contract and support staff (cleaners, drivers). Onboarding runs across the whole probation. The old way was a buddy system. Buddies cost people, so firms now want a platform with a checklist HR can see live.
- **What the platform he describes does.** AI builds a journey from each hire's job description. A countdown clock shows time left. Training videos and assignments in an LMS move a progress bar. HR sees it in real time and calls in anyone behind. This is course tracking, not wellbeing check-ins.
- **Why new hires leave.** About 90% of probation quits are personal, and mostly because they got a better offer. This is from his firm's case studies, not computed. Firing in probation is hard. Serious cases (for example harassment) go through the formal HR channel and become a recorded case.
- **Dashboard.** Layout is fine and easy to understand. Do not make it crowded. Group complaints into broad categories, not specific ones. He wants the root cause shown.
- **Close the loop.** Sending the fix to the manager is fine. He asked if AI drafts the message. It can. He asked how it is sent. By Telegram. He agreed: instant messaging beats email, because many people do not check email on a routine.
- **AI advice.** Show a weighted suggestion with a reason ("why"), not a flat suggestion. Weights must rest on real evidence, not invented. He named AI inventing sources (hallucination) as the key risk.
- **Sell it.** If it saves time (effective) and cost (efficient), it sells. Ask is it usable, easy, valuable.
- **More interviews.** Talk to HR people inside medium-sized firms that have a real HR department.
- **Competition.** He saw the dashboard and said it is quite similar to his. Abraham said Sapa is not for sale and offered to share everything. He agreed to see the code and interviews when Abraham open-sources them. He said he had not met a rival product until now.
- **Word to use.** "Autonomic": AI acts alone inside limits that a person sets. Buyers now want this, not plain "automated".
