# Client story: Contoh GBS, Penang

*As of 15 Oct 2026. This is the one source for the reference customer: who it is, why it needs Sapa, and every number about it. Other docs link here and do not restate it. The company is fictional. Each number is marked **Source** (real, with a link) or **Fictional** (chosen to fit the guide's scenario and the product). Never present a fictional number as research. An adversarial review on 15 Oct checked the arithmetic and the story; its fixes are applied.*

## Bottom line

Contoh GBS is a shared services centre in Bayan Lepas, Penang. It hires about 200 people a year: about 170 to replace leavers and about 30 for new work. More than 1 in 4 new hires leave before Month 6, most inside the first 90 days, before their training even ends. HR finds out why at the exit interview. The parent group has said the centre gets new work only if it keeps more of its new hires. Sapa is built for exactly this.

## The company (fictional)

- **Name:** Contoh Global Business Services (Penang) Sdn Bhd, "Contoh GBS". *Contoh* is Malay for "example", so the name is clearly fictional.
- **What it is:** the captive shared services centre (a back office owned by the group it serves) of Contoh Group, a fictional European maker of industrial pumps and valves in 14 countries. Penang serves 9 of them, in Asia and Europe.
- **Where:** a GBS office building in Bayan Lepas, opened 2017.
- **Staff:** 650.

| Payroll and HR operations for the Group | 30 | Payroll for 4 countries | |
| Management, quality and transitions | 30 | Team leads' managers, quality checks, moving new work in | |

- **Shifts:** Asia day shift (8 am–5 pm). Europe shift (3 pm–midnight, which is the European working day), about 40% of staff. A small US night shift on the IT service desk.
- **Site HR: five people for 650 staff, and only one works on new hires.** Kavitha (site HR manager: Group HR, the budget). Farah (recruiter: agencies and direct hiring, all week). Jason (HR business partner: promotions, pay reviews, performance). Daniel (HR admin: offer letters, EPF and SOCSO forms, IT access requests). Aishah (HR executive: onboarding, employee relations and every exit interview). HR works 8:30 am–5:30 pm. Its day ends 2.5 hours into the Europe shift, in that shift's busiest hours, so HR rarely talks to Europe-shift new hires after Day 1. Group HR in Europe owns the HR system and the engagement survey.
- **Team leads:** 38. Most were promoted from analyst within three years for being good at the work. The Group's people-lead course has a long waiting list, so most lead people for months before any training.
- **Hiring:** a monthly intake of 15–18 people on the first Monday, trained as one batch. About 60% come through three recruitment agencies. Each agency fee is 18% of annual salary, with a 90-day free replacement.
- **Probation:** 3 months.
- **Pay:** local analysts about RM3,000 a month. Language-desk hires about RM5,000. Language desks are about a quarter of hires, so the intake average is about RM3,500 a month (RM42,000 a year).
- **Training plan:** 3 weeks in a classroom, then 5 weeks beside a buddy. A new analyst works a full queue alone from about week 10–12.
- **System access:** a laptop, a company email, SAP and the ticket system arrive 3–5 working days after Day 1.
- **Languages:** staff are Malay, Chinese and Indian Malaysians, and some language-desk hires are foreign nationals. Day-to-day talk is English, Bahasa Malaysia, Manglish and, among Mandarin speakers, Chinese.

## Why it needs Sapa so badly (fictional, built on the sourced facts below)

1. **It bleeds new hires.** Of the 200 people in the intakes from October 2024 to September 2025, 54 left before Month 6 (27%). That is 73 of every 100 still there at Month 6. Total attrition rose from 19% in 2024 to 26% in 2025, above Malaysia's 18.2%. That is the year 8 new GBS centres opened in Penang, and GBS By The Sea opened down the road (point 4).
2. **Most leave inside 90 days, and the rest leave right after.** 32 of the 54 left within the first 90 days, most of them before week 10–12, so they never worked a full queue alone. Contoh paid for the training and got nothing back. A second wave of 22 left on Day 91–180, just after probation was confirmed and the agency's 90-day free replacement ran out. For an agency hire Contoh then pays a new fee.
3. **The leavers make more leavers.** Every exit leaves a queue that the rest of the team covers with overtime. Accounts payable met its invoice deadline in only 7 of the last 12 months. Overtime is one of the reasons people give for leaving.
4. **Down the road, someone is hiring.** Penang had at least 60 GBS companies in 2019, and 15 more centres opened in 2022–2025. GBS By The Sea opened in Bayan Lepas in August 2025 with AMD, Celestica and Microsoft as tenants, and more than 1,000 jobs expected. A new hire who is unsure about Contoh does not need to stay to find out.
5. **HR is blind for the first six months.** The Group's yearly engagement survey goes out by company email, in English, every March, only to staff with 6 or more months of service. So by design it never hears from an early leaver. Penang's survey scores describe only the people who stayed.
6. **HR hears the reason only at the door.** Aishah does a 15-minute exit interview on each person's last day and types notes into a spreadsheet. One weekend she read all 54 rows and grouped them by hand. The same five things kept coming up:
    - "I was never sure what to do first. Every country said their invoices were urgent."
    - "My team lead was always on calls with the country controllers. I couldn't ask anything."
    - "Nobody showed me SAP properly. I learnt from YouTube."
    - "On the Europe shift I never met the rest of my team."
    - "The agency said Finance Analyst. The job is invoice matching all day."

    These are the guide's own list: unclear priorities, weak onboarding, lack of support and feedback. Each row was read alone, months after anyone could act on it.
7. **New hires don't speak up early.** They are on probation, and the person who confirms it is the team lead they would complain about. Teams chat and company email are Group systems, and they do not arrive until Day 3–5 anyway.
8. **Team leads were never taught to onboard.** Raj, a 29-year-old team lead, saw his team double when the Group moved its Vietnam and Thailand payables to Penang in 2026. He got 12 new hires in six months while still learning to lead.
9. **The Group has set a deadline.** At its September 2027 review the Group will decide whether three more countries' finance work, about 60 roles, goes to Penang or to its other centre in Kraków. Its two conditions for Penang: six-month new-hire retention of at least 85% (today 73%), and accounts payable on deadline in 11 of 12 months (today 7). Kavitha must show by then that the trend is turning, why, and what she is doing about it.
10. **The Group reports turnover.** Group sustainability reporting asks each site every quarter for staff turnover and what it is doing about it. In Malaysia, Bursa's indicator C6(c) asks listed companies for the same. Today Penang can give the number but not the reason or the action.
11. **The agencies are hurting too.** An agency must find a free replacement for anyone who leaves inside 90 days, and most early leavers do. For anyone who leaves after that, it risks losing Contoh as a client. It wants to know which of its job adverts oversell the job.

## How the company fits the guide, step by step

| Guide (Challenge 4) | At Contoh GBS | What Sapa does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Several new employees leave within six months" | 54 of 200 in a year: 32 inside 90 days, 22 more by Month 6 | Check-ins from Day 3 to Month 6; 6 of the 7 fall inside the first 90 days, where most leavers go |
| "Exit interviews mention unclear priorities, weak onboarding and lack of feedback" | Aishah's 54 rows say exactly this | The five problem areas are these causes; the Impact view puts "top reasons leavers gave" beside what Sapa found early |
| "HR wants to understand problems earlier" | The Group survey never reaches anyone under 6 months; HR's day ends early in the Europe shift | Seven short chat check-ins on the new hire's own phone, any time, from Day 1 |
| Step 1: one employee group | Every new hire, all towers, Day 1 to Month 6 | Group card: everyone hired in the last six months |
| Step 2: voluntary feedback | Probation fear; no Group systems until Day 3–5 | QR code on Day 1; consent; HR only by default; skip, stop or delete any time |
| Step 3: AI summarises themes | 3,000–4,000 answers a year from about 100 people at a time, in English, BM, Manglish and Chinese | AI suggests a label with an evidence quote; SQL counts; AI writes the summary over the counts |
| Step 4: issues needing follow-up | One new hire in 54 might be in real trouble, not just unhappy | Flag queue; serious matters go straight to HR with a fixed safe message |
| Step 5: a tracked HR or manager action | 38 untrained team leads, most on calls much of the day | Drafted fix, assigned to the team lead in Telegram, Accept and Mark done, "Update from HR" to the new hires, recheck |
| Success question: "understand earlier and act before it becomes serious" | The Group's 85% target and the September 2027 review | Impact view: days from reply to fix, score change after fixes, six-month retention against the usual 73 in 100 |

**Why each product feature exists at Contoh:**

- **Telegram, not email or Teams:** the new hire has a phone on Day 1 and company email only on Day 3–5. A channel outside Group systems feels safer to someone on probation. The bot never sees their phone number unless they share it.
- **Why not WhatsApp (judge question):** Telegram's bot platform is free and needs no business account or message templates. Many Malaysians already have it. WhatsApp is the obvious next channel, and the product keeps the chat layer separate for that reason.
- **BM, English, Manglish and Chinese:** that is how Penang writes. Mandarin speakers in AP Asia and on the language desks can use the Chinese pack.
- **Handbook Q&A:** 15–18 new people a month ask the same things ("How do I claim overtime?"). HR has gone home for most of the Europe shift. Questions the handbook cannot answer ("Where can I park my motorbike?") show HR a gap.
- **Consent tiers and "5 for themes":** a new hire on a 6-person language desk can be identified by one quote.
- **Manager milestone nudges:** team leads promoted without training get the Day 1, 7 and 30 basics in Telegram.
- **Job-match card and agency view:** the agency advert says "Finance Analyst"; the job is invoice matching. The agency sees which roles oversell, pooled and never by name.
- **Fix evidence:** with 38 team leads trying different fixes, HR learns which ones work here.
- **Exit question:** a leaver who ticked it at consent can say why in one tap, so "top reasons leavers gave" is counted, not a weekend of reading.

## Money (for slide 10)

The cost per early leaver is **our estimate**, and it is one multiplication: 16% of a year's pay. A new hire earns about RM3,500 a month, so RM42,000 a year. 16% × RM42,000 = RM6,720, about **RM6,700**. The 16% is the typical cost of replacing a worker paid under US$30,000 a year, from the Center for American Progress review of turnover studies (Source, US, 2012). It covers recruiting, training and lost output.

| Item | Figure | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Average pay | RM3,500 a month, RM42,000 a year | Fictional; Penang customer-service jobs advertise about RM3,000–4,000 a month |
| Cost of one early leaver | about RM6,700 | 16% × RM42,000 = RM6,720 |
| Early leavers a year | 54 | Fictional |
| **All early leavers** | **about RM362,000 a year** | 54 × RM6,700 |

**Cross-checks.** Newer US studies give higher rates: 19.3% of pay for below-average wages (Source, US, 2020), which is RM8,100 here, and 33% of base pay (Source, US, 2025), which is RM13,900 here. We use the lowest, 16%, so the figure is hard to attack. The Malaysian Employers Federation's RM25,000–30,000 per leaver (Source, 2012, old) is the high case.

**"Even your base case misses the Group's 85%. Why buy?"** Reaching 85% means about 30 early leavers a year instead of 54. Sapa does not do that alone, and does not claim to. The fixes do the work: clear priorities, team lead training, honest job adverts, buddies on every shift. Sapa tells Kavitha which fix each team needs, weeks before the resignation, checks whether it worked, and gives her the evidence the Group asks for. Without it she has 54 spreadsheet rows and a guess. Nobody can prove one person stayed because of Sapa, so the pilot measures retention by intake, as a trend and not proof.

**HR time:** about 200 hires × 7 check-ins × 2–3 answers is 3,000–4,000 answers a year, from about 100 people at any time. Nobody reads that by hand. Test 3 measures the real saving.

## The pilot ask

A free pilot for every new hire in three monthly intakes (November 2026, December 2026 and January 2027, about 50 people), each through its own Month 6. The last one reaches Month 6 in July 2027, in time for the September review. Pass marks are agreed before it starts (dossier H2). With 15–18 people an intake, a single intake already passes the "5 for themes" rule. The three intakes together reach the 30 people the retention line needs.

**Be honest about what 50 people can show.** With 50 people, a retention figure is uncertain by about 12 points either way, so it cannot prove 73 against 85. It shows a direction. By September the review can also see the February to April 2027 intakes up to Day 90, and the Day-90 measures (response rate, days from reply to fix, score change after fixes) are much firmer.

## The demo is a future year

The demo is **not** a pilot result. It shows what Aishah would see one year into Sapa: six intakes are complete and the newest one is live. The dates in the seed are a stage device. ## Not found (do not claim)

- A current official count of Penang GBS companies or staff. The latest is 2019.
- Any attrition rate for Penang or Malaysian shared services, or any measured rate of leaving within six months. The 27%, the 73 in 100 and the 32-inside-90-days split are fictional.
- A measured share of six-month leavers who go inside 90 days. Say "the first 90 days matter most", not "most people leave within 90 days".
- Time for a shared services analyst to work alone. Contoh's 10–12 weeks is its own fictional training plan.
- Malaysia's current Employment Pass salary floor for foreign language hires. Check it before saying anything about foreign hires' pay.
- How big a raise makes someone switch, or how many leave over shift work.
