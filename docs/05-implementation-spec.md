## Rules (the source of truth for tests)

Each rule has an ID. Tests cite the ID. A rule changes only with team approval.

- **R1 Consent gate.** No check-in, reminder or recheck is sent to a person without an active, non-withdrawn consent. HR marks a person who left the company as "resigned" or "ended by the company". When HR does, all scheduled check-ins, reminders, rechecks, notices and offers for that person, and manager milestone nudges about that person (R45), are cancelled. The bot sends one fixed message: "Your check-ins have ended. Your answers are kept until [date]. Type DELETE to remove them now." ([date] is the R24 retention date.) Only the R49 exit question may follow, and only if the person resigned and ticked it at consent.
- **R2 Commands first.** Every command in R36 is matched before any AI call or safety check, in the language of any active pack (R46), case-insensitive. They work when the model is unreachable.
- **R3 STOP.** After STOP, no further messages are sent to that person except the STOP confirmation.
- **R4 DELETE.** After a confirmed DELETE, the person's name, replies, tags, follow-up records, chat state and chat ID are gone. Their contribution disappears from all live counts. Published weekly snapshots are not changed. One anonymous audit row records that a deletion happened.
- **R5 Default visibility.** A new consent has scope "HR only". The bot never asks who may read an answer.
- **R6 Manager read rule.** A manager can never read a reply's text. There is no API, screen or message that gives it to a manager.
- **R7 Escalation isolation.** Escalations are readable only by HR. Managers can never read or count them. Escalated replies are excluded from themes.
- **R8 Minimum group.** The organisation's minimum group size is 1 by default and can be set to any whole number of 1 or more. A theme count is returned only when respondents in the window are at least that size.
- **R9 Manager snapshots.** Managers see theme counts only from weekly snapshots, never live. Any count under 3 in a manager view shows as "fewer than 3".
- **R10 Action exposure.** An action is scoped to a team, an intake or one new hire. A fix for one new hire starts from one of their replies ("Send to their manager"). HR picks a fix from the library and one click sends it, with the fix's own fixed note and no AI, to that hire's own manager. "Draft a different fix" opens the editor instead. Its alert is not held for the weekly snapshot day, it quotes no snapshot count, and it names the hire ("This is about <name>, one of your new hires.") so the manager can act for that person. A reply from an anonymous hire (R62) gives "This is about one of your new hires." and HR sees "One new hire". It never carries the hire's words. Team and intake actions still wait until the next weekly snapshot day (R9) and need 5 in-scope new hires for that manager. The alert quotes the snapshot count when 5 or more named the area, else says "Fewer than 5". Demo mode skips the wait and shows the label "In real use this alert waits until Monday".
- **R11 Employee-facing text.** Every message the bot sends is fixed question or probe bank text, fixed system text, a resolution note that HR approved, a Q&A answer that passes R31, the person's own stored AI summary and tags shown under R40 (labelled as AI), the R39 offer and R29 closure texts that HR approved in settings, the R1 end-of-check-ins text, the R49 exit question, the R40 reply after a challenge, a fixed or AI follow-up question under R12 and R55, or the English rewrite of the person's own message under R57 (labelled as AI). Nothing else the AI writes reaches an employee. There is no AI summary to the employee and no "here is what I will tell HR" step.
- **R12 Follow-up caps.** A check-in has 6 taps and one optional open question (agreed 20 Oct 2026; 6 since 21 Oct 2026, when Onboarding and Training became two problem areas, and Belonging was renamed Team integration), so there are no follow-ups and no probe chats. The AI writes that one question from all 6 taps (it is the one AI-written opener R11 allows); the app shows the person's lowest taps above it and "Optional. Tap Skip…" below it. If the AI is unreachable or blocked, the fixed per-driver question is used. The rules below for an AI question still apply to it. A follow-up is fixed pack text when the reply fits R55, else an AI question. Code enforces the cap. Each AI question is one short, open, neutral question about a work situation. It never asks about feelings or mood, never suggests a cause, never asks for a name or contact, and never gives advice or a promise. Code blocks a question with a feelings word or a name or contact request, asks the AI once more, and ends the chat on a second block. A [Skip] or [Done] button ends the chat at any time. The keyword check runs on every message before the AI. No question after a reply that triggers escalation, by keyword or by the AI's flag.
- **R13 Escalation triggers.** A reply escalates if the keyword detector matches OR the model sets escalate. Either alone is enough. The keyword detector runs even when the model fails. The categories include `forced_labour_indicator` (passport or ID held by someone else, debt to an agent or a recruitment fee, restricted movement; wage deductions count only together with another sign). BM and English keywords ship with the app. Its keyword list holds only clear phrases, such as a passport held by the employer or agent, debt to the agent, or not being allowed to leave the hostel. Wage deductions alone are not a keyword: they go to the model, which weighs them with the rest of the reply. It goes to HR only, like every escalation (R7).
- **R14 Escalation reply.** On escalation the bot sends one short fixed message: thank you, this is serious, please report it to HR through the company's speak-up channel, you can stay 100% anonymous. Then it lists the organisation's general contacts, which hold only that channel (demo: web form, email and 24-hour phone). The same message goes out for every category. It never names 999, a helpline, the tribunal or any outside authority (docs/00). The bot asks nothing more: no contact question, no phone. HR sees each escalated reply and the count at once, with the hire's name, team and day. HR has no case steps, clock or closure in Sapa. Managers never see them.
- **R15 Evidence.** A tag is stored as "ok" only if its evidence quote is an exact substring of the masked reply after whitespace and Unicode normalisation. Otherwise retry once, then store as "needs review".
- **R16 No forbidden fields.** No stored AI output has an emotion, personality, sentiment score or quit-risk field. No tone or emotion feature is stored or computed from anything.
- **R17 Masking.** Malaysian NRIC numbers, phone numbers and email addresses in text are replaced with placeholders before any text leaves the app.
- **R18 Counts from SQL.** Every number shown in a theme card comes from SQL.
- **R19 Schedule.** Touchpoints come from the two check-in tracks (R60), counted from the day the person joined through the team QR (not the intake date), moved forward to the next working-hours slot on a working day in the organisation's time zone. Demo mode ignores working hours, for touchpoints and for R28 notices and R39 offers.
- **R20 Reminders.** One reminder 24 hours after an unanswered check-in. 24 hours after the reminder the check-in becomes skipped.
- **R21 Exactly-once delivery.** A scheduled check-in is sent at most once, even under concurrent dispatcher calls.
- **R22 Exactly-once processing.** An inbound chat update is processed at most once, even if the platform resends it.
- **R23 Synthetic people.** People marked synthetic are never sent real messages. No message about a synthetic person goes to anyone. This covers manager nudges (R45) and alerts.
- **R24 Retention.** Raw reply text older than the retention period after a person's last check-in is deleted by a nightly job.
- **R25 Audit.** Every server read of reply text and every change to tags, actions, consents, settings and roles writes an audit row with actor, time, and target.
- **R26 Recheck.** When an action becomes done, each consenting person in its scope gets, in their next check-in and as its first question, the same neutral scale question for the action's driver that the question bank asked before, with the same word buttons (for ROLE_CLARITY: "How clear are you on what is expected of you this week?", Not clear at all … Very clear, stored 1–5). The bank holds one such item for each driver, so every driver has an earlier score. HR may translate it but may not make it hint at the fix. The answer is linked to the action. "Recheck answered" counts only these answers. The action card leads with the difference: the average change of the people in scope who gave both answers, beside the average change of new hires outside the scope who gave both in the same weeks ("Role clarity, Raj's team +1.3, other teams +0.1 in the same weeks", with "2.5 → 3.8, 6 people" under it). The card always carries the fixed line "Teams picked for a fix often improve anyway. This compares them with other teams." With fewer than 5 people in scope who gave both, the card says "Not enough answers yet (N of 5)". With fewer than 5 outside the scope, it says "Not enough comparison yet (N of 5)".
- **R27 Team join link.** HR gives one join link and QR per team in each intake, not per person (docs/00, "Privacy"). HR hands them out; team leads never do. The HR screen "Join codes" shows one QR per team, labelled with the team name and its team lead, with a copy-link button, and the line "Hand these out yourself, for example on each team's Day 1 welcome sheet. Do not ask team leads to hand them out." A switcher swaps every card to a second QR that links the team lead's own phone to Sapa as that manager (`/start mgr-…`); once linked the card shows "Phone linked". The demo reset makes a new lead token for each manager that is not linked (R59). A token works for anyone until the intake's expiry date. Each `/start <token>` from a new chat creates a new person with a random code name, the intake's start date, and that token's team and manager. The bot asks language, then consent, then named or anonymous (R62), then one tap "Is this your first full-time job?" (R61), and never asks for a team. The bot never stores the Telegram name or username. A chat that has already joined gets the fixed "You have already joined Sapa on this phone" text in its language. Sapa asks for a name only when the hire picks "Share my name" (R62), and never for a phone (R14). HR can no longer mark one person as left, so R1's left flow and R49 apply only to seeded data. The per-person `addHire` API stays only as a test fixture; no screen uses it.
- **R28 Resolution notice.** When an action becomes done and has an HR-approved note, the bot sends the note, inside a fixed wrapper, to every person in the action's scope who has active consent and has not stopped, deleted or left. It is sent in each person's language, in the next working-hours slot, once per person. The note for a language is the HR-approved note. The manager's outcome note is never sent. A dismissed action sends nothing. A hiring-side action (R52) sends nothing. An action cannot be assigned without an HR-approved note in English and in Bahasa Malaysia, unless HR ticks "no notice" with a reason.
- **R29 Escalation closure.** Retired 16 Oct 2026. Official complaints are handled outside Sapa, so Sapa sends no closure message (docs/00).
- **R30 One Q&A call.** A text that arrives when no check-in is open, or after `/ask` (`/tanya`), goes through commands (R2) and the keyword safety check (R13) first. Then one structured model call receives the published handbook (placed first, for prompt caching), the Q&A memory (R33) and the masked message (R17). It returns the fields in "Q&A output" below. `/ask` forces `kind = question`. If `kind = greeting` (a hello, thanks or "who are you"), the bot sends one fixed text that says what it can answer, stores nothing and logs nothing for HR. If `kind = feedback`, the text is stored as an unprompted reply and goes through the normal classify step, then is thanked with fixed text. If the model is unreachable, the text is stored as an unprompted reply marked "awaiting AI" and the bot sends a fixed "got it" text.
- **R31 Grounded answer.** An answer is sent only if `cited_path` is a published handbook page in the asker's language, or in English or Bahasa Malaysia when the asker's language has no published page, and `supporting_quote` is an exact substring of that page's text after markdown formatting is removed and whitespace and Unicode are normalised. The sent message has the "AI answer" label, the answer, the supporting quote shown as "From the handbook: …", the page path and the HR contact line. When the page is not in the asker's language, the model answers in the page's language and the bot adds that pack's fixed text: "This answer is in [English]. Ask HR if it is not clear." No new AI words reach the employee. Otherwise the bot sends the fixed "I don't know yet" text, which says the question was logged for HR without the person's name, and the question is stored with outcome *unknown*.
- **R32 Important matters.** If `important_topic` is set, or the keyword check matches, the bot sends that topic's fixed channel message (HR contact only) and no AI answer. The topics are: harassment, bullying or discrimination; safety hazards; self-harm or crisis; pay problems (pay that is missing, wrong or disputed; questions about how pay or overtime normally works are answerable); probation, warnings, dismissal or resignation; medical or health matters; legal questions. Those that also match R13 open an escalation. The others are logged with outcome *important*, without the person's name, and are never escalated.
- **R33 Q&A memory.** The model sees at most the last 6 Q&A messages from the last 10 minutes for that person. Check-in replies are never included.
- **R34 Grouping and anonymity.** Each question joins the group the call returned (an existing group ID or a new group with neutral text). A group is marked forwarded when it reaches 3 distinct askers in 30 days. HR sees a group's neutral text, its distinct askers in the last 30 days and its outcomes once it has been forwarded, or at once when the handbook could not answer it (so HR can fill the gap and draft a fix from it). Smaller answered groups show only as one total ("5 other questions from fewer than 3 people"). Questions on an R32 important topic are never grouped by text; HR sees only a count per topic, at 3 or more. HR never sees who asked or the asker's own words. Managers never see questions.
- **R35 Questions and privacy.** Questions follow DELETE (R4) and retention (R24). A deleted person stops counting toward any group.
- **R36 Commands.** The commands are `/start`, `/ask`, `/skip`, `/language`, `/stop`, `/delete`, `/myanswers`, `/privacy`, `/help` and their BM forms `/tanya`, `/langkau`, `/bahasa`, `/berhenti`, `/padam`, `/jawapansaya`, `/privasi`, `/bantuan` (`/start` is shared, for the QR deep link). Each Telegram chat's menu is set to its person's language with chat-scoped `setMyCommands`; Every command in every active pack language is accepted from everyone. Typed STOP, BERHENTI, DELETE and PADAM, without the slash, act like their commands. A Telegram "bot was blocked" error when sending counts as `/stop`.
- **R37 Fallback model spend cap.** Every model call records the model, tokens and estimated cost. When the fallback model's total estimated cost passes $1.50, the fallback is skipped and the request goes straight to "awaiting AI". The primary route has no cap in code.
- **R38 One model per eval.** Eval and reliability runs name exactly one model and run with the fallback switched off. A call that fails is recorded as failed, never answered by another model.
- **R39 HR offer.** On a reply that the AI or HR marked as needing follow-up (`concern_level = needs_follow_up`; HR can set or clear it, with an audit row, R25) and that is not an escalation, HR may send one fixed offer in the person's language: "HR would like to help with what you shared. Would you like a short private chat? [Yes] [No thanks]". The text is HR-approved in settings, not AI-written. A person has at most one open offer. At most one offer per reply, and no new offer for 30 days after "No thanks". The offer is sent in the next working-hours slot (R19). It is blocked if the person stopped, deleted or left, and HR then sees only the generic reason in R41. "Yes" creates an HR contact task due in 2 working days; "No thanks" closes it. No answer in 7 days closes it. Managers never see offers. The offer is allowed under "HR only" consent, because only HR acts.
- **R40 Own answers.** `/myanswers` sends the person their last 10 stored replies, each with its date, the AI summary and the problem-area labels in the person's language, under an "AI reading" label. The classifier writes `summary` in the reply's language, and R40 shows that one. Where HR changed a label, the label shows "Changed by HR" in place of "AI reading". Each has a "This is wrong" button. A press marks that reply's tags `needs_review` with source "challenged by new hire" and takes them out of counts until HR reviews them. When HR resolves the challenge, the bot sends the fixed text "HR checked your note. The label now reads: …". Escalated replies are listed with date and text only. Escalation details and HR notes are never shown. It works only for the person's own chat.
- **R41 Participation visibility.** HR sees the replies people wrote as "Name · team · Day N" (the name is what the person typed after consent; "A new hire" until they give one), never a code name, person ID or start date, in any HR screen or API response. Phone numbers, emails and ID numbers in reply text, evidence quotes and translations are masked (R17) in what HR sees. Managers never see a name, a code name or a reply. HR never sees a list of who missed, skipped, declined or stopped, and no per-person timeline. Every blocked HR action to a person who stopped, declined, deleted or left (R29, R39) shows the same one reason: "This person is not receiving messages from Sapa now." It never says stopped, declined or left. A person's data export goes to the person, needs a reason from HR, and is audited.
- **R42 Retention and impact.** Six-month retention for an intake is the people still employed at Month 6 divided by everyone hired in that intake, whether or not they joined Sapa. After the intake passes Month 6, HR enters both numbers from its own records, for each agency that placed people in it and for hires with no agency. Sapa stores no per-person leaver date for this figure, so DELETE (R4) does not change it. The Impact view pools all completed intakes and shows "k of n" against the baseline. Below 30 people it says "Too few to tell yet (n of 30)". It always says "This shows a trend, not proof that Sapa caused it". The per-person left flag (R1) never feeds this figure. Other Impact figures (response rate, days from reply to done, score change against other teams after done actions) are shown per intake and only for 5 or more people. They count live data, so R4 removes a deleted person from them and never from retention.
- **R43 Fix evidence.** Each action library entry stores the score difference (R26) of every done action that used it in this organisation: the change of the people in scope minus the change of new hires outside the scope in the same weeks. The draft screen shows "Used 3 times here. Role clarity rose 1.0 more than in other teams (41 people)." It shows the difference only when, counted across the entry's done actions, 5 or more people in scope and 5 or more outside gave both answers. With fewer than 5 outside it says "Not enough comparison yet". With fewer than 5 in scope it says "Not enough evidence here yet". A new draft starts from the top entry ranked by the difference, not by the raw rise. Library fixes use no AI; the AI drafts only a new fix that HR names and asks for. The Impact view lists the three fixes with the largest difference, with the line "a trend, not proof".
- **R44 AI quality watch.** Each week the review queue gets a random sample of AI-confident tags per driver and language (3, or all there are), marked "Spot check". HR mostly reviews tags the AI was unsure about, so the changed share is computed on spot checks only. A dashboard panel shows, for the last 30 days, the share of spot checks HR changed and the number of `/myanswers` challenges, per driver and per language. AI-confident tags count without review from the start. If HR changed more than 20% of the spot checks for a driver in a language (at 10 or more), AI-confident tags for that driver and language stop counting until HR reviews them. The card says why. The hold-new-tags setting (HR admin) holds all new tags in the same way at any time. The panel also shows escalation false alarms and the share sent to review, per language, and "Not evaluated" for a pack whose test has not passed (R46).
- **R45 Manager milestones.** For every new hire in a manager's team who is still with the company, the manager gets fixed nudges in Telegram from an HR-approved library at set days: Day 1 "Assign a buddy", Day 7 "Book a 15-minute priorities chat", Day 30 "Agree a 30-60-90 plan". Each has a Done button. A nudge contains no feedback and no reply data. It is sent whether or not the hire joined, answered, stopped or deleted, so a nudge never shows who took part. It stops when HR marks the hire as left (R1) and is never sent about a synthetic hire (R23). It follows R19 working hours. Demo mode skips working hours but sends no milestone nudge, so a clock jump does not fill the phone on stage. HR sees the share done per intake (5 or more hires), never per manager. The Impact view shows it beside retention.
- **R46 Language packs.** A language is a pack: consent text, question bank, probes, commands, fixed texts (including the R31 note) and safety keywords. Three packs are built: Bahasa Malaysia, English and Chinese (Simplified). Manglish (mixed BM and English) is not a pack: the BM and English packs both take it. Telegram commands can use only Latin letters, so the Chinese menu lists the English commands with Chinese descriptions. All three packs are on for every organisation from creation, with no setting to turn one on or off. A pack's tags count without HR review only after the pack has its own test set (at least 30 replies and at least 20 escalation cases, forced-labour cases among them) and passes the marks in "Evaluation method". Until then every tag in that language goes to "needs review", and the R44 panel shows "Not evaluated". HR writes handbook pages (R31) in BM and English, and Chinese where HR chooses; a Chinese asker without a Chinese page gets the R31 fallback. Employee notes (R28) are written in all three languages; the AI draft gives all three for HR to approve.
- **R47 Worker-voice export.** HR can export, per intake, the theme counts and top question topics as "worker-voice themes for your RBA or ESG report". It holds counts with their base, only at 5 or more people. It also holds the number of serious concerns received and acknowledged within 1 working day, shown as "fewer than 5" below 5. It never holds replies, names or escalation details. Each export writes an audit row (R25).
- **R48 Agency view.** The role `agency` is granted by the employer per agency, for all that agency's placements. An agency user sees themes, the job-match card (R52) and retention (R42) for the people that agency placed, pooled across all its placements. It sees only counts of 5 or more (retention from 30, as in R42) and never personal data. It never sees replies, questions, escalations, names or actions.
- **R49 Exit question.** The bot does not ask for a tick at consent any more, so this applies only to seeded people who have the tick. When HR marks such a person as left, as "resigned" (R1), the bot sends one fixed question with driver buttons and one open line. The answer goes through the safety check (R13) and is tagged like a reply, with source "exit". It never counts in check-in themes and never goes to a manager. The Impact view shows, at 5 or more answers over 12 months, "Top reasons leavers gave" beside "Top themes Sapa found earlier" for the same intakes (all hires, not only leavers). A person marked "ended by the company" is not asked. STOP, DELETE and R4 apply as always. Retention (R42) does not use this mark.
- **R50 Voice notes.** *Removed. Voice notes are out of scope. A voice note, image or file gets a fixed text that asks the person to type, and nothing from it is stored.*
- **R51 Teams that need help, never a league table.** HR may see which teams have a problem area more often than the company, so a fix reaches the right team lead. The list uses the latest weekly snapshot (R9), never live counts. A team is listed when at least 5 of its new hires answered and its count is at least 2 more than expected. Expected is the team's answered count times the company rate for that area. Listed teams show in A–Z order, each with its count, its average 1–5 score for that area, the company rate, the company average score and its team lead. They are never sorted by count. Teams under 5 in the same tower are pooled into one tower row, which follows the same test. If the answers outside every shown row would add up to fewer than 5, the smallest shown row is hidden too, so no hidden group can be worked out by subtraction. A team view never splits further by intake or role. The panel says how many teams it lists, out of how many have 5 or more answers, and how many it hides and why. Managers never see any team but their own. No screen, export or message shows teams or managers in ranked order. Managers are told this in their "What I can see" note.
- **R52 Job match.** The `people` table holds each person's `role` (job title). The question bank holds the item "does the job match what was described when you were hired" (dossier F4). For each role, over the six-month group, HR sees a "Job matches what you were told" card, for example "Accounts payable analyst: 12 of 26 said 'not really' at Day 14". A role with fewer than 5 people shows "Not enough people yet (N of 5 needed)" (R8). Managers never see the card. Its library entry is "Update the job ad and the interview script for this role". Its action is hiring-side: HR owns it and may name the recruiter in its text, it is never assigned to a line manager, it names no person, and it sends no notice (R28) and no recheck (R26). The agency view shows this card for its placements (R48).
- **R53 HR translation.** A reply, question or challenge written in Chinese is shown to HR as written, with an AI translation into the HR working language beside it, labelled "AI translation". The evidence quote is highlighted in the original. The translation is made when HR opens the item, is never stored, never shown to a manager or a new hire, and never counted or classified. Classification always runs on the original text.
- **R54 One fix for many teams.** From the R51 list, HR can send one fix to several teams at once. HR writes the title, the steps, the new-hire note (English and Bahasa Malaysia, R28) and the due date once. Sapa makes one action per team. Each action is scoped to that team, owned by its team lead, and linked to the others as one batch. Each action is checked on its own against every assign rule, R10 included. A team that fails is not sent, and HR sees why. The other teams still go. Each team lead sees only their own action. Each action gets its own recheck result (R26). The Fixes page shows a batch as one row, with how many teams accepted it and how many finished.
- **R55 Chat check-in.** **Superseded 20 Oct 2026 by R12: 6 taps, then one optional AI-written question with a [Skip] button; no probe chats, no follow-ups. The probe and follow-up text below is dormant.** Each check-in starts with the fixed intro: "A few quick taps so HR can see if things get better for your team." and the privacy line from docs/00. Scale questions show five word buttons that fit the question, one per row, worst first, and no numbers. Sapa stores 1–5 (1 = worst). A typed label or a typed digit counts as a tap. A tap of 1 or 2 on one of the five driver questions starts a probe chat at once, for the first 2 such taps of a check-in (none in a recheck check-in, R56). The bot sends a fixed opening question for that driver from the pack (R58 puts `You tapped "<label>".` in front), with a [Skip] button that goes on to the next tap. A third low tap, or the end of the taps, gets the fixed "What is happening?" after the last tap; with every low tap already probed it gets the fixed "Anything that would make next week easier?". Follow-ups (R12) aim at one real example, how it affects work, and what would help. A follow-up is fixed pack text only when the reply names the driver (a pack word for that driver in the person's language or English), has no pay, health, legal or advice word, no question mark, no "fine"/"okay"-type word and at most 25 words; the text is "Can you give one example from this week?" after a reply of 3 words or fewer, else "How does that affect your work?", then "What would make it easier?". Any other reply, and every reply to a chat with no driver, goes to the AI. The chat stops when those are met, the person signals done, the reply is positive with nothing to dig into, 2 replies in a row hold no work detail (the AI's flag, and code counts a one-word or letter-free reply as none), or the person goes off topic a second time (the first time the AI steers back once). A keyword hit (R13) or the AI's own serious-concern flag ends the chat at once with the R14 message and escalates. A pay, probation, health or legal question, or a request for advice, goes to the AI and gets the fixed "please ask HR" line, and the chat may go on. A keyword hit or serious-concern flag in a probe chat escalates that message and the taps go on. The person's words from every probe chat and the final chat are stored as one reply, classified once (R15), and shown to HR as one reply. The chat uses its own model (`CHAT_MODEL_ID`); every other AI call keeps its model. If the AI is unreachable, a probe asks the next fixed follow-up, and the final chat ends after the first answer with the R37 behaviour.
- **R56 Fix follow-up.** In a recheck check-in (R26) for an action that had an approved note to new hires (R28), the bot asks, after the other taps, "Since the last Update from HR about <topic>, how has it changed for you?" with Much worse · A bit worse · No change · A bit better · Much better (stored 1–5, linked to the action). An action with "no notice" and a hiring-side action ask nothing. <topic> is a short fixed noun for the fix's driver, from the pack, in the person's language (en: your role · support from your manager · your team · your training · your workload). The chat then opens on that tap: `You tapped "<label>". What changed?` after 4–5 and `You tapped "<label>". What is still not working?` after 1–3 (the R58 words plus the fixed question), with the R55 follow-up and stop rules. The chat reply is linked to the action. The action page keeps the R26 score change as the main proof. Under it, "What they say" shows the count per answer word only from 5 answers, else "Not enough answers yet (N of 5)", then up to 3 evidence quotes from the linked chat replies, and the fixed line "People often say things got better when asked. Compare with the score change above."
- **R57 Help me say it.** `/say <text>` (`/cakap` in Bahasa Malaysia; Chinese uses `/say`), or `/say` followed by the next message, rewrites the person's own message as clear, polite English they copy and send to their lead themselves. With no text the bot sends the fixed prompt "Type the message you want to send to your lead, in any language." The keyword check (R13) runs first. A hit gets the R14 Speak Up message, the same escalation as an unprompted message, and no rewrite. Otherwise the text is masked (R17) and one structured call with the default model (not `CHAT_MODEL_ID`) returns English of at most 80 words with the same meaning. It adds no facts, names, promises or advice, keeps placeholders such as [PHONE], and treats the text as data. Code checks for valid JSON, non-empty text and the length cap; it retries once, then sends the fixed "Sorry, I cannot help with that right now. Please try again later." The reply is the label "AI suggestion. Check it before you send:" with the text, then the fixed "Copy it and send it to your lead yourself. Sapa does not send or keep it." (en, ms, zh, R46). Important topics (R32) may be rewritten, with no advice added. Sapa stores nothing: no `responses` row and no text in logs or `ai_usage` (a row with token counts and call type `say` is allowed). Nothing reaches HR or the manager, except a keyword hit, which is escalated like any unprompted message.
- **R58 Say what was tapped.** Every inline button tap (check-in word, contact choice, skip, stop, language, consent, manager accept or done, exit, delete) is answered in the person's language by the fixed words `You tapped "<label>".` (`Anda tekan "<label>".`, `你选了“<label>”。`), where <label> is the button's own text. The words open the first reply of that tap, in the same message and before its text and buttons, so the person never sees them twice or as a separate message. A tap that gets no reply gets none. A typed answer is not a tap and gets no such line. The share-phone-number key is not an inline button and gets none.
- **R59 Demo reset keeps phones and join links.** The demo reset ("Reset to seed" and `POST /api/demo/reset`) works the same in the demo and in tests. Each team's join token is the same after every reset (an HMAC of `DEMO_RESET_SECRET`, the intake and the team), so a QR on a slide keeps working. A team lead whose phone is linked stays linked. A new hire who joined through a team link and gave consent is put back on the same team, in the same language, with consent and a new check-in schedule from the intake's start date. Test files that need no linked phones unlink them before the reset.
- **R60 Check-in tracks.** HR keeps two tracks in the dashboard (Check-in schedule, admins only): "New hire" and "Employee". Each has days after the join date and a list of questions from the bank (the six drivers and the open chat). New hire starts as Day 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 90 and 182 with every question. Employee starts as Day 273, 365, 456 and 547 with manager support, team integration, workload and the open chat. A day is in one track only, and each track keeps at least one day and one question. A person's join date is the day they join through a team QR, in the organisation's time zone. Saved days apply to people who join afterwards. Saved questions apply to every check-in not yet sent: the questions are fixed on the check-in row when it is sent. The recheck question (R26), the job-match question and the fix-change question (R56) still apply.
- **R62 Named or anonymous.** After consent the bot asks "Should HR see your name?" with [Share my name] and [Stay anonymous]. Named asks the name. `/privacy` (BM `/privasi`) shows the current choice and the same two buttons. Each reply keeps the choice the hire had when they sent it, so a switch never changes old replies. HR sees an anonymous reply, in the reply list and the review queue, only when at least 5 different people in its team have a reply tagged (ok or reviewed) with one of its problem areas; until then it is held and its byline never shows a name. Counts include every hire. HR cannot send a private-chat offer (R39) from an anonymous reply. Serious concerns (R13, R14) are shown as now, with no name.
- **R61 First job split and repeat flag.** After consent the bot asks one tap, "Is this your first full-time job?" (Yes / No, all three languages). The answer sits on the person as a yes/no and no screen, reply row, export or API shows it with a person. The problem page splits each problem area into "First job" and "Experienced" from the same SQL counts as every other split, so a group under the minimum group size (R8) shows only "Not enough people yet". A team view never splits by it (R51). When a team is listed for a problem area in the latest weekly snapshot (R51) and was also listed in an earlier episode within the last 6 months, the problem page says "2nd time in 6 months" (3rd, 4th and so on). An episode is a run of consecutive weekly snapshots in which the team had the minimum group size and was at least 2 above its expected count for that week's company rate. This is SQL counts from the weekly snapshots, never the AI. With no earlier episode the page shows nothing.

## Testing Decisions

**What a good test is here.** Every test is an end-to-end test of a real user action on real sample data. It acts as a new hire on Telegram, as HR or a manager on the API, or as Supabase Cron. It sends the same HTTP request that user's client sends, to the running app. It checks only what that user, or the next user, sees: the messages the bot sends, the HTTP status and body, the rows in the database, the count on a card. Tests exist only for the rules R1–R57 above and for bug fixes. Expected values come from this spec's rules, the seed numbers below and the dossier, never from reading the code. Any test that uses time freezes the clock (see "Clock in tests").

**Not allowed.** These are fake tests and are deleted on sight:
- Calling a module function, server action or route handler directly from the test, instead of over HTTP.
- Faking, stubbing or spying on any part of our own code: the database, Supabase Auth, the clock module, the AI pipeline, the safety check or the Chat SDK adapter.
- Checking that an internal function ran, or how many times.
- Data made up inside the test file. Tests use the seed and the data files.
- A test that still passes when the handler under test returns an empty 200 and writes nothing.
- A real model key or a real Telegram token in the test run.

**The test stack.** One command runs it, from a clean state:
1. A local Supabase stack with every migration applied.
2. The demo seed loaded from the data files: Contoh GBS, the 100 hires, the handbook, the question groups and the seed tags. Each test file resets to the seed first, with the same reset the demo uses.
3. The app as a real Next.js production build (`next build` then `next start`), with the same routes, middleware and server actions that deploy to Vercel.
4. Two fake outside services, each a small local HTTP server. Only these two are faked:
   - **Fake model server.** Speaks the OpenAI-compatible chat API. `OMNIROUTE_BASE_URL` and the DeepSeek base URL point at it. Each test scripts its answers: valid JSON, invalid JSON, a timeout, a wrong evidence quote, a made-up number. The app's real provider code, Zod parsing, retry, fallback chain and `ai_usage` writes all run.
   - **Fake Telegram Bot API.** The Chat SDK Telegram adapter's API base URL points at it. It records every `sendMessage`, `setMyCommands` and `answerCallbackQuery` call, and can return "bot was blocked" (R36). On day 1, check that the Chat SDK adapter accepts a custom API base URL. If it does not, log it in the report and pick the smallest way to send its calls to the fake server in the test environment only.
   When the test environment flag is set, the app refuses to start if any base URL is not on localhost.

**How each user acts in a test.**
- **New hire.** The test posts a Telegram update JSON (text, command or button press, with a new update ID) to the real webhook route, with the webhook secret header. It reads what the bot said from the fake Telegram server. The update bodies are copies of real Telegram updates, stored as data files.
- **HR, HR admin and manager.** The test signs in through Supabase Auth as the seeded user and calls the real API routes and server actions with that session cookie, the way the dashboard will.
- **Supabase Cron.** The test calls the dispatch route and the nightly job route over HTTP with the shared secret.

**Clock in tests.** The app runs as a separate process, so the test cannot inject a clock object. When the test environment flag is set, the clock module reads a frozen "now" from one row that the test writes before each step. That code path does not exist outside the test environment. The demo offset still works on top of it, as in production.

**Seams.** Three. Almost all tests use seam 1.
1. **User journeys through the running app (main seam).** As above. Covers R1–R5, R10–R15, R17–R37, R39–R56.
2. **Database as a signed-in role.** Tests sign in as an HR user, an HR admin and a manager, and query tables directly with that session through the Supabase client, the way a browser could. They prove the second wall holds even if server code is wrong. Covers R6, R7, R8, R9 at the database level.
3. **Eval harness with the real model.** Not part of the test run. Runs the classifier on the labelled set, the Q&A answerer on the Q&A set, and the reliability set, and writes results files. Each run names one model, with the fallback off (R38). Covers the quality claims, R16 and R31–R32 in practice.

**Required journeys.** At least these, each as one test file named by its rule IDs. Each step is a real user action from the list above.
1. **Join and consent** (R1, R5, R27, R36). A new hire opens the intake deep link (`/start <token>`), picks a language and consents. The team and manager come from the link. The bot menu is set in that language. Consent scope is "HR only". A second chat on the same link becomes a second person. The same chat a second time is refused. A token after the intake's expiry date is refused.
2. **Day 14 check-in, the demo loop** (R11, R12, R15, R17, R18, R19, R21, R22, R26, R28, R44). The live hire is at Day 13. The test moves the clock to Day 14 and calls dispatch twice at the same time: one check-in is sent. The hire replies with a role-clarity reply from the labelled set, which includes a phone number. The fake model server receives the masked text. The test resends the same update ID: nothing new happens. The card goes from 23 of 64 to 24 of 65 who answered as soon as the reply is tagged (R44), and Confirm label moves it from AI-confident to reviewed. The Korean desk's team view says "Not enough people yet (4 of 5 needed)". HR drafts the action from the theme card, approves the note in English and Bahasa Malaysia, assigns and completes the action. Every in-scope consenting hire gets the notice once; the next check-in has the neutral 1–5 question as its first question (R26).
3. **Escalation** (R7, R13, R14). A hire sends a crisis reply while the fake model server times out. The keyword check still escalates, the bot sends the safe message with the configured contacts, and HR is alerted. The manager sees nothing, and the reply is not in any theme count. A hire who writes that the agent holds her passport escalates by keyword, and the fixed reply holds the outside labour-authority contact, which HR cannot remove in settings. A hire who writes only that EPF and SOCSO were taken from the pay does not escalate by keyword.
4. **Model down** (R2, R30, R37). The fake model server fails for both providers. Commands still work. A reply is stored as "awaiting AI" and the bot sends the fixed "got it" text. With seeded fallback spend over $1.50, the fake DeepSeek endpoint gets no call.
5. **Q&A** (R30–R34). Using the handbook seed: "How do I claim overtime?" gets a grounded answer with the quote and page. A question with a quote not in the page gets "I don't know yet". A pay dispute gets the important-matter message and no AI answer. A third distinct asker forwards a group, and HR then sees it, without names.
6. **Stop, delete, own answers** (R3, R4, R35, R40). After STOP, dispatch sends nothing to that hire. After a confirmed DELETE, the hire's rows are gone, live counts drop by one, published snapshots do not change, and one anonymous audit row exists. `/myanswers` shows the hire's own replies; "This is wrong" takes them out of HR counts.
7. **Manager view** (R6, R8, R9, R10). The seeded supervisor sees only weekly snapshot counts and "fewer than 3" for small counts. No manager API or screen returns reply text, even for a hire who tapped every button.
8. **Manager alert timing** (R9, R10, R39). With demo mode off, HR tries "Draft action" on a reply by a "HR only" hire: it is blocked and points to the theme card. HR assigns an action from the theme card: the manager gets no Telegram alert until the snapshot day, and the alert quotes the snapshot count only. With a snapshot count of 2, the text does not say "several". HR sets "needs follow-up" on the reply and sends the offer: it goes out in the next working-hours slot, a second offer to the same person is refused, and after "No thanks" a new offer within 30 days is refused.
9. **Recheck and fix evidence** (R26, R43). A done action sends the neutral 1–5 question as the first question of the next check-in. The card shows "Not enough answers yet (1 of 5)". The seeded earlier action shows "Role clarity, Raj's team +1.3, other teams +0.1 in the same weeks", "2.5 → 3.8, 6 people" and the fixed line about teams picked for a fix. The draft screen for "Priorities chat and written top-3 list" shows "Used 3 times here. Role clarity rose 1.0 more than in other teams (41 people)". An entry with fewer than 5 people in scope shows "Not enough evidence here yet"; one with fewer than 5 outside shows "Not enough comparison yet". Every driver has a 1–5 item in the question bank.
10. **Left the company and exit question** (R1, R41, R49). HR marks a hire as resigned: dispatch sends nothing but the fixed end text with a retention date, and the supervisor gets no more nudges about that hire. A seeded hire who has the exit tick gets one fixed question, and its answer is not in any check-in theme and not visible to the manager. A hire who did not tick gets none. A hire marked "ended by the company" gets none, even with the tick. HR then tries an offer to that hire and sees only "This person is not receiving messages from Sapa now". The same text shows for a hire who stopped.
11. **Impact and AI quality watch** (R42, R44). HR enters hired and still-employed numbers for an intake and an agency. Below 30 people pooled, the Impact view says "Too few to tell yet (n of 30)"; the seed shows "80 of 99". A hire confirms DELETE: the retention figure does not change. The nightly job puts 3 AI-confident tags per driver and language in the review queue, marked "Spot check". HR changes 3 of 10 spot checks of ROLE_CLARITY in one language: AI-confident ROLE_CLARITY tags in that language stop counting, the card says why, and the same driver in another language is not affected. In a new language, AI-confident tags wait for HR until 20 spot checks with at most 10% changed.
12. **Manager milestones** (R1, R23, R45). The supervisor gets the Day 1, 7 and 30 nudges for every non-synthetic hire in his team (the test seed adds some, bound to the fake chats), including a hire who stopped,, with the same text, on Telegram. He gets none for the five synthetic hires and none for a hire HR marked as left. In demo mode he gets none. Nothing in a nudge contains reply data. HR sees the share done per intake and no per-manager figure.
13. **Languages** (R36, R46, R53). A hire in a new organisation is offered all three languages. A hire who picks Chinese gets the consent text in Chinese and a menu of English commands with Chinese descriptions. HR opening that hire's Chinese reply sees the original and a labelled AI translation; no translation row is stored (R53). A voice note gets the fixed "please type" text and nothing is stored.
14. **Agency, export and no ranking** (R47, R48, R51). An agency user sees themes, the job-match card and retention only for that agency's placements, pooled over its intakes. A pool of 4 people shows "Not enough people yet", and retention below 30 shows "Too few to tell yet". Another agency's people never appear. The agency user gets a refusal on a reply, a question or an escalation. The worker-voice export holds counts only and writes an audit row. The team list for a problem area holds only teams with 5 or more answers that are at least 2 above expected, in A–Z order, from the latest snapshot. A team under 5 never appears alone. A manager gets a refusal on the list.
15. **Job match and fallbacks** (R31, R52). The seeded Accounts payable analyst card reads "12 of 26 said 'not really' at Day 14". A role with fewer than 5 people shows "Not enough people yet", and a manager gets a refusal on the card. HR drafts the hiring-side action from the card: it cannot be assigned to a line manager, and completing it sends no notice. A hire who asks in English about a topic that has only a Bahasa Malaysia page (test seed) gets the answer from that page with the fixed note "This answer is in Bahasa Malaysia. Ask HR if it is not clear."
16. **One fix, many teams** (R54). HR sends one library fix to three listed teams. Three actions exist, each scoped to one team and owned by its lead, in one batch. Each lead gets only their own alert. A team whose lead has fewer than 3 in-scope hires, on a held fix, is not sent, and the reply says why. The other teams still go.
17. **Chat check-in** (R11, R12, R55). The check-in shows the privacy intro and word buttons. A typed word counts as a tap. A low tap gets its fixed opening question at once; a reply that names the driver gets a fixed follow-up and any other reply gets an AI follow-up, at most 2; a question with a feelings word is asked again; HR gets one reply with an exact quote. A keyword or the AI's flag ends the chat with the Speak Up message and no question. A pay question gets the ask-HR line. Two replies with no detail end the chat. With no low tap the light question comes, and Done ends the chat. Skip in a probe goes on to the next tap.
18. **Fix follow-up** (R26, R56). After a done fix with a note, the next check-in starts with the neutral question, asks the fix question after the other taps, and opens the chat on it. A fix with no note asks no fix question. The action page shows the answer words only from 5 answers, the quotes, and "Recheck answered" counting only the neutral answers.

**Proof each test is real.** Before a test counts as done, break the rule it checks by hand (for example, remove the dedupe on update ID) and rerun it. It must fail. Then restore the code. Record this once per test file in the morning report.

One end-to-end rehearsal script runs journey 2 against the deployed app, with a scripted Telegram update to the real webhook, and checks the final action state and the recheck message. It runs before each rehearsal, not on every commit.

**What is not covered.** The dashboard pages (Abraham builds them; they call the same routes the tests call). Real Telegram delivery and the real model, which the phone test and the eval cover.

**Prior art.** None. The repo has no code yet. The first tests set the pattern: one test file per journey, named by rule ID.

**Evaluation method (slide 8).**
- Replies written by one teammate are labelled by the other two, who did not write them. Gold labels are agreed after.
- Split 30% dev for prompt tuning, 70% test for the reported numbers. The test split is run once per prompt version.
- κ per driver as yes/no, then the mean; per language (BM, English, Chinese and Manglish).
- A pack counts without HR review only after its own test set (at least 30 replies and at least 20 escalation cases, forced-labour cases among them) passes the marks in this list (R46). Until then it reports "Not evaluated".
- Precision per driver and per language for the tags that count without review (medium or high confidence, not uncertain). The cut-off is set on the dev split. The share sent to review is reported with it.
- Follow-up recall, and escalation recall as "k of n" with a 95% interval, over at least 40 escalation cases (8–10 give an interval too wide to support "tuned to miss nothing"). The set includes forced-labour cases in BM and English, and a false-alarm set of normal deductions (EPF, SOCSO, tax, lateness) that must not escalate.
- Reliability: share of first-try valid outputs over 100 runs; safe handling of injection, empty, gibberish and emoji-only replies.
- Time to insight: the tester did not plant the themes.
- Q&A: 30 synthetic questions (15 answerable from the seed handbook, 8 not covered, 7 important matters, some in Manglish). Targets: 0 answers on important matters; correct page and a passing quote on at least 90% of answerable questions; "I don't know yet" on at least 90% of uncovered questions.
- Escalation false alarms and needs-review rates are reported beside recall, per language. A Manglish writer should not get more red flags than an English writer. The test split includes 10–15 Manglish and BM hyperbole cases ("OT everyday, mati la") that must not escalate.
- Probe choice: agreement of `probe_id` with a human choice on the labelled set. Baseline: always send the generic probe. Pass if humans prefer the AI's probe in at least 60% of pairs.
- Draft action: in Test 4, how much HR changes each draft (unchanged, light edit, rewrite). Target: at least 70% unchanged or light edit. Baseline: the raw library text. Pass if fewer AI drafts need a rewrite than raw library texts.
- Q&A faithfulness: on the 15 answerable questions, a human checks that the answer says what its quote says. The quote check alone cannot catch a wrong rate beside a real quote.
- Recheck, score change, fix evidence, spot checks and the job-match card use no AI. They are tested as rules (R26, R43, R44, R52).
